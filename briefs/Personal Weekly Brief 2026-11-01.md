# Personal Weekly Brief, 2026-11-01

## Books and Reading

### 1. An Unkindness of Ghosts, Rivers Solomon (lead pick)
- **Type:** Novel, Black science fiction, generation-ship story (debut)
- **Date:** Akashic Books, 2017 (page counts differ by edition, 340 or 352)
- **Source:** Akashic Books; NPR review; Publishers Weekly
- **Availability:** Check catalog
- **Why for you:** The HSS Matilda carries the last of humanity toward a promised land, and its decks are sorted by race. The lower decks hold the laborers, the upper decks the white-supremacist crew. Reviewers read it as American slavery transposed to the stars. Aster, a gifted healer, starts uncovering the ship's secrets. It is a Lambda Literary Award finalist and got Solomon a 2018 Campbell Award for Best New Writer nomination. Publishers Weekly warns it leans on metaphor over hard science, which suits a reader who likes her SF political. Solomon is new to these briefs, and has a backlist if this lands.
- **Connects to:** Wild Seed (last week) for the power-and-bondage thread, and Space Is the Place below for the way Black SF imagines leaving.

### 2. An Extraordinary Union, Alyssa Cole
- **Type:** Historical romance, book one of the Loyal League series
- **Date:** Kensington, March 28, 2017 (paperback)
- **Source:** Kensington Books; Publishers Weekly; named to Kirkus and Publishers Weekly best-of-2017 lists
- **Availability:** Check catalog
- **Why for you:** Elle, a formerly enslaved woman with a photographic memory, goes undercover in a Confederate household as a Union spy, and meets Malcolm McCall, a Pinkerton agent. It ticks romance, historical fiction, and Black history in one book, and it is a bingeable series (A Hope Divided and An Unconditional Freedom follow). Hoopla lists the series, but I did not verify Greenville's holdings.
- **Connects to:** Fire, Sword, and Sea and Where the False Gods Dwell from earlier weeks, for Black women as historical protagonists.

### 3. Washington Black, Esi Edugyan
- **Type:** Historical fiction, adventure
- **Date:** 2018 (Serpent's Tail in the UK; Patrick Crean Editions in Canada). Sources disagree on the US publisher, so confirm your edition.
- **Source:** Booker Prize library; Serpent's Tail; Giller Prize coverage
- **Availability:** Check catalog
- **Why for you:** An eleven-year-old enslaved boy on a Barbados sugar plantation is taken up by his master's brother, a scientist and inventor, and ends up in a hot-air balloon escape and a trail across the world. It is part slave narrative, part Victorian science adventure, with a speculative-feeling edge. Shortlisted for the 2018 Booker Prize, winner of the 2018 Giller Prize. Edugyan is Canadian with Ghanaian roots.
- **Connects to:** An Unkindness of Ghosts, for science and bondage, one in the past and one in the future.

## Watch

### 4. Space Is the Place, John Coney (starring Sun Ra)
- **Type:** Feature film, Afrofuturist science fiction (about 81 to 85 minutes, sources vary)
- **Date:** 1974 (one archive lists 1978, but most sources say 1974)
- **Source:** Criterion Channel listing, also tracked by JustWatch
- **Availability:** Available elsewhere
- **Why for you:** Sun Ra returns from space to offer Black Americans a new planet, and takes on a rival at a Black youth center in Oakland. It is strange, funny, and the clearest early screen statement of Afrofuturism. It also pairs with The Last Angel of History from the earlier brief. Aggregators list it on Criterion Channel and HBO Max, but check the Criterion page before subscribing.
- **Connects to:** An Unkindness of Ghosts, as two very different answers to leaving Earth.

## Learn

### 5. Digital Schomburg, Schomburg Center for Research in Black Culture (NYPL)
- **Type:** Digital archive, free exhibitions, photographs, books, audio and video
- **Date:** Ongoing
- **Source:** nypl.org Schomburg Center pages; NYPL Digital Collections
- **Availability:** Freely available online
- **Why for you:** One of the great Black archives, open for browsing, including a project with digitized books by 38 nineteenth-century African American women writers. Counts of digitized material vary by source (over 1,000 collections, 17,000 items, and 1,100 collections are all quoted), so treat the scale as large rather than exact. Good for a long rainy-weekend wander and a source for the history behind your historical fiction picks.
- **Connects to:** Colored Conventions Project (earlier pick), another digital archive of nineteenth-century Black life.

## Rabbit Hole of the Week: Sun Ra and the Arkestra

Watch Space Is the Place, then listen to the 1972 album of the same name that the film grew from, and follow the thread back to Sun Ra's Chicago years and forward to Afrofuturist music (Parliament, Janelle Monae). For the written side, Rivers Solomon's novel is a good literary partner to the film's argument that Black people might build a world elsewhere. Browse the Schomburg for the Chicago and Harlem Black press context that shaped him.
