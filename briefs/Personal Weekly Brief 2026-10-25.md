# Personal Weekly Brief, 2026-10-25

## Books and Reading

### 1. Wild Seed, Octavia E. Butler (lead pick)
- **Type:** Novel, Afrofuturist science fiction, first in chronology of the Patternist series
- **Date:** Doubleday, 1980 (fourth Patternist book published, but first in the story's timeline)
- **Source:** First edition record from a rare-book dealer; Wikipedia's Patternist publication order
- **Availability:** Check catalog
- **Why for you:** Anyanwu, a Nigerian shapeshifter and healer, is pulled across centuries and an ocean by Doro, an immortal who breeds people like livestock. It is the cornerstone of Afrofuturism: West African origins, the slave trade, power and survival, all told as a duel between two near-gods. It is also a way into a whole backlist. Readers say the Patternist books can be read in any order, and Wild Seed is the natural place to start. Butler has not appeared in your earlier briefs, which is a surprising gap.
- **Connects to:** Rabbit Hole below.

### 2. Lagos 2060, curated by Ayodele Arigbabu
- **Type:** Short fiction anthology, eight Nigerian science fiction writers imagining Lagos in 2060
- **Date:** DADA Books, 2013
- **Source:** Unglue.it campaign page; Strange Horizons feature on the writers; BSFA Vector review
- **Availability:** Available elsewhere
- **Why for you:** A workshop-born book that asks what the city will be like, written by the people who live there. It sits well beside Okorafor's Lagos in Lagoon from last week. Note that the unglue.it page lists a far-off "ungluing" date (2059), so do not count on a free copy; look for a bookseller or Afrelib.
- **Connects to:** Lagoon (last week), as the same city from many hands.

### 3. Fire, Sword, and Sea, Vanessa Riley
- **Type:** Historical fiction, dual timeline, based on the pirate Jacquotte Delahaye
- **Date:** William Morrow; a retailer pre-order listing says January 13, 2026, but a bookseller page left the date blank, so confirm
- **Source:** William Morrow listing (ISBN 9780063271043); Book Riot list of 2026 historical fiction
- **Availability:** Check catalog
- **Why for you:** Caribbean piracy, a woman who may or may not have existed, and a novelist known for her Afro-Caribbean historical fiction. A different corner of Black history from the American South and the Katherine Dunham voyage of recent weeks. Riley has a backlist, including Queen of Exiles, if it lands.

## Watch

### 4. Pumzi, Wanuri Kahiu
- **Type:** Short film, about 22 minutes, Kenyan science fiction
- **Date:** 2009 (premiered at the Kenya Film Festival, screened at Sundance 2010)
- **Source:** HapaKenya; African Film Festival; University of Florida climate-imagination pages
- **Availability:** Available elsewhere
- **Why for you:** Kenya's first science fiction film, set in a post-water-war future where a woman tries to grow a plant. You already have Kahiu's TEDxNairobi talk on your list, so this is the work behind the argument. I could not confirm a permanent free stream. Past online screenings have expired, so search for current options.
- **Connects to:** Afronauts (earlier brief) for another short film that makes a lot with little.

## Rabbit Hole of the Week: Butler and the scholars who read her

After Wild Seed, the trail is Strange Matings: Science Fiction, Feminism, African American Voices, and Octavia E. Butler, edited by Rebecca J. Holden and Nisi Shawl, which collects essays on Wild Seed and Kindred, including Luisah Teish's account of finding Wild Seed. For the larger picture, the Bloomsbury Handbook to Octavia E. Butler ties her work to Afrofuturism, race, and gender. Then circle back to the Smithsonian and MSU material from last week and see where Butler sits.
