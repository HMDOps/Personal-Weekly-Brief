# Personal Weekly Brief, 2026-10-18

## Books and Reading

### 1. Lagoon, Nnedi Okorafor (lead pick)
- **Type:** Novel, Africanfuturist first-contact science fiction
- **Date:** Hodder & Stoughton, 2014; Saga Press (US), July 14, 2015
- **Source:** Simon & Schuster / Saga Press; Los Angeles Review of Books essay on Okorafor's literary Nigeria
- **Availability:** Check catalog
- **Why for you:** Aliens land in the water off Lagos, and the city, not Washington or London, is where first contact plays out. The aliens say plainly that they do not want to rule or conquer, and Okorafor wrote it partly as an answer to the way District 9 pictured Lagos. Okorafor is back on the list with a different work (Binti and The Daughter Who Remains were earlier picks) because this is her best-known standalone Lagos novel and it pairs with the TED talk below.
- **Connects to:** The Okorafor TED talk below, where she uses excerpts from Lagoon.

### 2. The Rosewater Insurrection, Tade Thompson
- **Type:** Novel, Africanfuturist science fiction, book two of the Wormwood trilogy
- **Date:** Orbit, March 12, 2019 (UK paperback listed March 14, 2019)
- **Source:** Orbit / Hachette; Kirkus
- **Availability:** Check catalog
- **Why for you:** Rosewater, book one, was an earlier pick, so this is the bingeable next step. The trilogy ends with The Rosewater Redemption. If you liked the Nigerian city grown around an alien biodome, this is where the politics tighten. A strong weekend-series candidate.
- **Connects to:** Lagoon. Two very different Nigerian alien-arrival books, one in Lagos, one in a city built around the alien.

### 3. Keeper of Lost Children, Sadeqa Johnson
- **Type:** Historical fiction
- **Date:** February 10, 2026 (Large print edition dated March 1, 2026)
- **Source:** Library and bookseller records list 37 Ink / Simon & Schuster; one retailer lists Berkley, so the imprint is not settled
- **Availability:** Check catalog
- **Why for you:** The wife of a Black American soldier in post-WWII Germany meets mixed-race orphans left behind by the occupation. It is a lesser-told corner of Black American history, from a novelist who writes strongly about women and family. Johnson also has a backlist, including The House of Eve, if it lands.

### 4. A Girl Like Her, Talia Hibbert
- **Type:** Contemporary romance, book one of the Ravenswood series
- **Date:** March 3, 2026 (Sourcebooks). An Australian Penguin listing says April 1, 2026, probably a different edition or distributor.
- **Source:** Sourcebooks; Swooon list of best Black romance books of 2026
- **Availability:** Check catalog
- **Why for you:** Hibbert is a British Black romance author with a sharp, funny voice. This is the start of a new series, so it is also a possible binge. The lead character, Ruth Kabbah, is a recluse in a small town after a bad experience with her boss, so expect warmth with some bite.

### 5. When Trees Testify, Beronda L. Montgomery
- **Type:** Nonfiction, science, history, and memoir on Black botanical knowledge
- **Date:** Henry Holt, January 20, 2026 (only a retailer listing gave the exact date, so confirm)
- **Source:** Henry Holt; reported as a Library Reads notable nonfiction pick
- **Availability:** Check catalog
- **Why for you:** Your gardening lane, from a plant scientist. It pairs with Conquer the Soil (already recommended) but from the lab and the memoir side.

## Watch

### 6. Sci-fi stories that imagine a future Africa, Nnedi Okorafor (TEDGlobal 2017)
- **Type:** Talk, about 9 minutes
- **Date:** August 2017 (Arusha, Tanzania)
- **Source:** TED.com
- **Availability:** Freely available online
- **Why for you:** The shortest possible statement of why Africanfuturism exists: science fiction as political writing, built on the question "What if?" Watch it before or after Lagoon.

## Learn

### 7. Afrofuturism: A History of Black Futures, Smithsonian NMAAHC (online companion)
- **Type:** Museum exhibition web presence, with a companion book edited by curator Kevin M. Strait
- **Date:** Physical show ran March 24, 2023 to August 18, 2024; earlier press gave a March 2024 end date, so I used the museum's own page
- **Source:** nmaahc.si.edu/afrofuturism
- **Availability:** Freely available online
- **Why for you:** More than 100 objects across music, film, comics, fashion, and literature, from Sun Ra to OutKast to P-Funk. Good for browsing between novels. I did not open the pages, so how much is online is not confirmed.

## Rabbit Hole of the Week: Voices of the Black Imaginary

Michigan State University holds an oral history archive of interviews with Afrofuturism scholars and creators, led by Julian Chambliss and held by the Vincent Voice Library. Interviewees include Chesya Burke, Tenea D. Johnson, Reynaldo Anderson, Ingrid LaFleur, and Iheoma Nwachukwu. It grew out of the Zora Neale Hurston Festival's Afrofuturism cycle (2020 to 2024). Start with the NMAAHC objects, then listen to the people building the field. The Every Tongue Got to Confess podcast, which Chambliss co-produces, is a side trail. I could not confirm how much of the archive is publicly open, so check the MSU Digital Library.
